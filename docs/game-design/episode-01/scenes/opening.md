# docs/game-design/episode-01/scenes/opening.md

# Scene 0: Little Star

## Status

**Status:** **Accepted** by the creative director. Crafted with the `craft-scene` workflow from the director's answers. Director's words are quoted. The dream frame is now `vision.md` decision 9; the meeting is recorded in `world.md`.

**Length target:** 2–3 minutes of the 10–15. The opening earns the loneliness and the meeting; it does not rush to the first system.

## What Sofi feels

Director's words: *"Sofi is amazed when she sees the RF telescope and remembers that she saw such in a cartoon. The cartoon gives the first impulse to check what this is in real life."* And: *"curiosity, she saw the same in the cartoon."*

Amazement first, then the itch to get closer. Not fear. The dark is a puzzle, not a threat.

## The real place

VIRAC, Irbene, Latvia. The RT-32: a fully steerable radio dish 32 metres across, about 600 tonnes, on a roughly 25-metre concrete tower, rising above flat coastal pine forest and visible from far away. The site's historic code name was "Little Star". A 16-metre dish (RT-16) stands nearby. Sources: `corpus/viraclatvia/facts.md` [V1][LP][CW].

## What is invented

- The dish is dark and broken. In reality it works. The game shows the place as it was in its broken years after 1994, inside Sofi's dream (`fiction-boundaries.md`).
- The gate sign reading "Little Star" in the game's language. The real code name existed; the sign is ours.
- Sofi's route: she got off the school bus a stop early to cut through the forest and saw the dish over the trees. Told in one line, not shown.
- Pluto being found here. Where Pluto came from is not said and not implied. Both readings stay open.
- Night. Sofi is seven and alone at night because this is a dream. The dream frame is a series-level decision; see *Flag for the director* below.

## Entry

Black. Forest sound: wind in high pines, one owl far off. A quiet piano motif, four notes, unresolved.

Fade in on the forest floor at Sofi's eye height (1.0 m). Pine trunks, moonlight in stripes. She is walking toward something the player cannot see yet; the camera is hers, the player has control from the first frame.

The trees open. The dish.

It fills the sky. Moonlit metal, the tower below it in shadow, the bowl tilted up as if it were listening. This is the shot the whole episode is built around and it comes first. The piano stops on the sight of it. Wind only.

Proposed first line (for the director to accept or rewrite):

> **Sofi:** "That's... that's the one from the cartoon. The alien one. It's *real*."

Beat. Then, running:

> **Sofi:** "I saw you from the bus!"

She runs. The player runs with her. Grass, a broken fence with a gap a seven-year-old fits through, a rusted gate half open. On the gate, a metal sign, letters half gone. If the player stops, Sofi sounds it out the way a first-grader does:

> **Sofi:** "L... i... little. S-t... star. Little Star. That's a *good* name."

This is optional; running past it costs nothing.

## The discovery

The tower door at the base of the dish is open. Inside: nothing. Not dark like night, dark like a closed box. Sofi stops at the threshold. This is the first puzzle: **getting in**. The door is not locked; the darkness is.

> **Sofi:** "Hello? ... It's too dark. I can't see the floor."

She will not step into a dark she cannot see the bottom of. Neither would the player. The critical path is blocked by nothing but the absence of light, and there is no lamp, no switch, no power. Nothing in the world can solve this yet.

Then the meeting.

Something on the floor just inside the door catches the moon. A puddle. Silver, not water. It moves when Sofi moves; when she steps back, it stops. Spooky for exactly one breath, then curious.

Director's steer: *"a meeting phase vibe"* — two beings who do not share a language, careful, then trusting. Influence, not copy.

The meeting is a tiny communication puzzle and it teaches the game's core grammar before Sofi knows it:

1. Sofi taps the doorframe once. The puddle ripples once.
2. She taps twice. It ripples twice.
3. The player taps three times (interact button ×3). It ripples three times, and rises off the floor into a shape about Sofi's height. A quicksilver figure, no face, standing very still.

> **Sofi:** "You copied me!" *(delighted)* "Do it again."

It does. She laughs. The meeting is over; they are friends. She names it on the spot, the way a seven-year-old names a stray:

> **Sofi:** "You're all shiny and round. You're Pluto."

(Pluto, the planet she likes best because it was "kicked out and I think that's mean" — a line for later, not here.)

Nothing explains what Pluto is. Pluto does not speak in this scene. Repetition was the whole conversation, and repetition is how the receiver will talk to them later.

## Pluto's form

**Puddle** (arrival), then **Walker** (standing), then the first tool form: **Lens**.

Sofi looks at the dark doorway, then at the moon, then at Pluto. The player holds interact on the doorway with Pluto near; Pluto is offered one form. Pluto flows up the doorframe and flattens into a curved silver disc that catches the moonlight and throws a soft beam down the corridor. The floor appears. Dust, a stair, a cable on the ground like a sleeping snake.

This is the first learning beat, **dark and light**, delivered as a tool: light can be caught and pointed. No text. Sofi says the whole lesson:

> **Sofi:** "You made the moon go *in*."

## The change

- The corridor is lit by a single moving moonbeam that follows Pluto's angle. The dead palette stays cold; the beam is the only warm-ish thing, and it is still silver.
- No system has woken. The score does not gain a layer. Instead, a second quiet instrument answers the piano motif for the first time: Pluto has a sound (a soft ripple tone, synthesised). The world now has two voices.
- The tower interior is enterable. The generator shed and the ground floor become the next scenes.

## Optional depth

- The **Little Star** sign: Sofi reads it aloud if the player stops. Pluto, if already met, ripples once at the word "star". Nothing more. (Both readings of Pluto survive: a mimic, or something that knows the word.)
- The **small dish** (RT-16) is visible through the trees to one side. Sofi: "There's a baby one." A player who walks to it finds the first of the observer's notes weighted under a stone. No text on screen; Pluto cannot read yet; Sofi pockets it. It is read later in the workshop.
- The **cable on the floor** at the corridor entrance is the same cable Sofi will bridge with Pluto as Conductor in the Power scene. Seen now, understood later.
- **Spooky but kind:** a moth circles Pluto's lens-beam. Sofi: "He thinks you're a lamp." That is the only creature in the scene besides the owl.

## Audio

- Forest ambient loop (wind, one owl). Room tone changes when Sofi crosses the threshold.
- Piano motif: four notes, stops on the first sight of the dish, returns very quietly when Sofi laughs.
- Pluto's ripple tone: synthesised, one pitch, repeated to match taps. This is Pluto's voice for the whole series. It is not the Voice's timbre; the two must be distinguishable at once (`world.md`, sound design constraints).
- No rhythm layer yet. Rhythm enters with Power, per the GDD plan.

## Out

Sofi steps into the moonbeam. Pluto flows down from the frame and reforms beside her as Walker. She looks back once at the dish through the door, then forward into the dark stair.

> **Sofi:** "Okay. Let's find the aliens."

Pull: the cartoon in her head, and now a friend to do it with. The generator shed is the next scene.

All within `art-direction.md` class budgets.

- Environment: pine trunk modules ×3 variants, forest floor, moon skybox or star field (decision pending in `art-direction.md`).

## Flag for the director: the dream frame

Director's words: *"maybe this is a dream, she is actually dreaming and having deep connection to stars and universe. This makes it easier to do interesting things. At the end of the episode she wakes up. In another episode we do not play this dream game."*

This is a series-level framing decision and belongs in `vision.md` (general decisions) and `world.md`, not in a scene. I have written the scene assuming it. Before I add it to the vision, one concern and one suggestion:

- **Concern:** if Episode 1 ends with "she wakes up" and nothing crosses over, the returning tone and the pull south can feel undone. The player needs the pull to survive waking.
- **Suggestion:** she wakes, and one small thing is real. Options: she is humming the three-tone count without knowing why; the word "south" is written in her school notebook in her own handwriting; or she simply gets off the bus at the early stop again the next day. Pluto is not in her room. Whether Pluto was only a dream is the held question for the series.

Also note: the dream frame is why she is alone at night, and it lets later episodes skip the frame entirely, as you said. It does not need to be explained in the opening. A single visual cue is enough; I would rather the opening not show a bedroom at all and let the ending reveal it.
