# Scene 2: The Dish Turns

## Status

**Status:** **Accepted** by the creative director. Director's words quoted. Two rooms plus a gallery; one lock; no new controller mechanics beyond typing two digits. The artefact principle is now `vision.md` decision 10.

**Length target:** 3–4 minutes. The longest scene so far, because it carries the episode's spectacle.

## What Sofi feels

Curiosity turning into the first real "I figured it out". Director's words: *"the learning is in the artefacts — working or not working for entering the computer."* And for the payoff: *"she initially doesn't understand, there is some sound; then another perspective, like the roof."*

Wonder at the end: 600 tonnes moving over her head because she typed a number.

## The real place

RT-32 is fully steerable: the 32-metre, ~600-tonne dish turns on drive motors to face any point in the sky. It is nicknamed "Saturn" and "the Big Dish". A radio telescope does not look with light; it turns to *listen* at a point in the sky and records what it hears. First EVN observations succeeded on 12 April 2012 after Latvian engineers rebuilt the control and drive systems without documentation. Sources: `corpus/viraclatvia/facts.md` [V1][EVN][R1].

## What is invented

- Two rooms at the top of the stair: a **drive room** (motors, gears, the dish's mount overhead; visual only, no actions) and a **control room** (desk, dead screen, artefacts). Real VIRAC has drive and control spaces; their layout here is ours.
- The **lock**: the observer's computer asks for a two-digit number. The observer hid it in a magazine crossword. Director: *"a magazine with an article... with some crossword puzzle that could open the control computer, like the password was encrypted in the article's puzzle."*
- The **magazine** is an invented popular-science monthly, no real title, no real masthead. Its article about the Big Dish uses real facts.
- The acid is gone. Director: *"not play the acid game anymore."* The drive works once told to.
- The **gallery**: a small outside platform reached from the control room, under the dish. Ours.

## Entry

The lit stair ends at a landing with two doors and a hatch.

### The drive room (visual, no actions)

The left door is heavy and already open. Inside is the biggest indoor space of the episode: the underside of the dish's mount. Motors the size of cars. A gear wheel taller than Sofi, teeth like steps. Chains, a rail, a smell of old oil (Sofi wrinkles her nose). Everything still. Stair bulbs from Scene 1 light it in pools; the rest is shadow.

> **Sofi:** "This is the *inside* of the telescope? It's all wheels."

She walks under it. The camera at 1.0 m makes the gear a wall. On the motor housing, a brass plate: **RT-32**. Sofi reads it letter by letter if the player looks at it:

> **Sofi:** "R. T. Three. Two. RT-32. Is that its name? That's a robot name."

The spider is on a gear tooth, findable.

Nothing to do here yet. The room exists so the player knows what will move.

### The control room

The right door. Smaller, warmer even when dead: a desk, a chair too big for her, a screen with a blinking green square in the corner (power is on; nothing else). Shelves, a wall of switches, a window onto the drive room. Things everywhere: the observer left in a hurry.

## The discovery

### The lock

Sofi climbs onto the chair. The screen wakes at her touch: a drawing of a padlock and two empty boxes. `_ _`. No words. A keypad on the desk with numbers 0–9.

> **Sofi:** "It wants two numbers. Which two numbers?"

She tries her age. The screen wobbles side to side like a head shaking, gently, and the boxes clear. No sound of failure, no lockout. The player can try any numbers, any number of times.

### The artefacts

Director's principle: *"exploring artefacts — they reveal interesting and useful facts and this flows through the entire game. Some help, some don't."* Each artefact in the room can be looked at. Each gives one line. Together they teach that reading tells you things. Only one path is required; three lead to the answer.

| Artefact | What it gives | Helps? |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **The magazine**, open on the desk to a crossword | The observer filled most of it in pencil. One row is boxed in red. Clue: *"The Big Dish across, in metres."* The row is blank. In the margin, the observer's hand: *"password = this."* | Yes: the question |
| **The article**, two pages before the crossword | A photo of the dish under construction (no faces). Pluto reads the first paragraph aloud. It says the dish is thirty-two metres across, the biggest in Northern Europe. | Yes: the answer |
| **The brass plate** in the drive room | RT-32 | Yes: the answer, for a player who connects it |
| **A calendar** on the wall, stopped on one month | Sofi tries the day number. Wobble. She laughs. | No, kindly |
| **A mug** with a faded star on it | Sofi: "Somebody drank from this a lot." | No; character |
| **A star chart** pinned on the wall, hand-annotated, one point circled | Pluto ripples once at it. Sofi: "What's *that* one?" No answer. | No; a seed for the receiver scene |
| **A tape recorder**, dead, a cassette inside | Sofi presses play. Nothing. "It's asleep." | No; a seed for the receiver scene |
| **The two notes** Sofi pocketed in Scenes 0 and 1 | Pluto can now read them: the loop diagram with the X; a line about "the road up" | Optional depth; continuity |

### Pluto's first words

When Sofi puts the magazine in front of Pluto, Pluto reads. Out loud. Slowly, evenly, like someone reading in a second language.

> **Pluto:** "The Big Dish is thirty-two metres across. It is the largest in Northern Europe."
> **Sofi:** *(staring)* "You can *read*?"
> **Sofi:** "...You can *talk*?"

Pluto ripples. That is the whole answer. Both readings of Pluto survive: a friend who has been quietly listening and learning, or something that already knew. Not addressed.

### The answer

Sofi looks at the crossword, at the boxed row, at the article. Counts on her fingers what "across" means. Looks through the window at the plate.

> **Sofi:** "Thirty-two. It's *called* thirty-two. It's *thirty-two big*. Thirty-two!"

She types 3, 2. The padlock opens. The screen fills with one thing: a big round dial and one word-free button, an arrow curving up.

If the player stalls for a long time (tuning: about 90 seconds after the magazine is first opened), Pluto walks to the window and stands looking at the brass plate until the player follows. Critical path stays passable; nobody is stuck.

### Turn

Sofi presses the button.

## The change

### The sound (she doesn't understand yet)

A deep sound from the next room. Not loud. A groan of metal that has not moved in years, then a low, steady grind. The window glass buzzes. Dust falls from the ceiling in the control room.

> **Sofi:** "...What did I do? Pluto, what did I do?"

Director: *"she initially doesn't understand, there is some sound."*

She runs to the drive room door. The gear is turning. Slowly, tooth by tooth, the wall of teeth walks past her. The motors hum. Chains move. It is enormous and it is *working*.

> **Sofi:** "It's moving. The whole thing is moving. Where is it going?"

### The other perspective

The hatch on the landing. It was closed; now, with the drive running, a small lamp above it is lit (light group). Pluto flows up and becomes **Ladder**; Sofi climbs, pushes the hatch, and comes out onto the **gallery**: a narrow railed platform on the tower, under the open sky.

Above her, the dish is turning. The whole 32-metre bowl, silver-grey in moonlight, tilting up and around, the red rim light from Scene 1 sweeping a slow arc across the stars. The sound is different out here: a far-off hum and the wind. The forest below, the small dish beside them, the moon.

This is the second hero shot of the episode. The player can just stand.

> **Sofi:** "It's looking at the sky."
> **Sofi:** "...It hasn't got an eye. How does it look?"

Pluto ripples. No answer yet. That question is the receiver scene.

Director: *"we just make the dish turn. No more in this episode."* The dish tilts to the sky and settles. It does not point at anything in particular. It is awake.

Score: the strings enter (GDD plan: third layer). The place has a voice now, not only a pulse.

## Pluto's forms

**Ladder** (the hatch). Reading is a behaviour, not a form. Lens optional in the control room for the dark corners.

## Optional depth

- **Every artefact** in the table above; the magazine has more pages (a second article, on stars, read by Pluto if asked).
- **The notes** from Scenes 0 and 1, now readable.
- **The star chart's circled point.** No payoff here; remembered in the receiver scene.
- **The spider** on the gear tooth, and again on the gallery rail after the turn.
- **A player who never reads anything** can still type 32 from the plate; a player who reads the article gets Pluto's first words earlier.

Retroactive note for Scene 0 (director's suggestion): the opening can carry one or two artefacts too, in the same spirit: the sign, and a note under a stone are already there; a third (a child's drawing of the dish taped inside the tower door: someone else once thought it was an alien machine too) is proposed for the next pass on `opening.md`.

## Audio

- Drive room: silence with the faint hum from below; drips.
- Control room: screen wake tone (soft), keypad clicks, wobble sound (a gentle "mm-mm"), unlock (one clear chime).
- Pluto's reading voice: calm, even; recorded, not synthesised, but processed to sit near Pluto's ripple tone.
- The turn: metal groan → steady grind → hum. Window buzz. Dust.
- Gallery: wind, far hum, the red light sweeping makes no sound.
- Score: strings enter as the dish begins to tilt.

## Out

Sofi stands on the gallery a long moment, then looks down at the hatch.

> **Sofi:** "If it's listening, we have to hear what it hears."

Pull: the tape recorder and the star chart below are waiting, and the dish is up there listening to nothing yet. Scene 3 is the receiver.

## Assets this scene adds

Within `art-direction.md` budgets.

- Set: drive-room gear wheel (set piece), two motor housings, chain/rail, brass plate (decal); control desk, chair, screen (emissive UI texture ×3 states: lock, wobble, dial), keypad, switch wall, shelves, window.
- Small props: magazine (two spreads: crossword, article; localisable textures), calendar, mug, star chart, tape recorder + cassette, hatch + lamp.
- Environment: gallery platform + rail; sky visible (star field decision pending in `art-direction.md`).
- Hero asset: RT-32 needs a rotation rig (azimuth + elevation) and the red rim light emissive, both already implied by Scene 1.
- Pluto: Ladder.

## Implementation notes

- The lock is a two-digit input; wrong input triggers a wobble animation; correct input swaps the screen state. No text parsing.
- Dish rotation is a scripted animation on the hero asset; gear/chain rotation in the drive room is driven by the same clock so they visibly agree.
- Hatch lamp is a light group triggered by the drive state.
- Reading is a "look at" interaction that plays a voice line; no text box is required for progress, but subtitles are shown by default per the platform plan.
