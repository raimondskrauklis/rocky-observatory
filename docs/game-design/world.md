# World — Characters, the Voice, the Language, the Real Sky

## Status

**Status:** Base draft — iterate. Derives from `vision.md` (v2). Sample lines are proposals for the creator to accept, rewrite, or reject.

**Decides:** who the characters are and how they behave; what the Voice is and is not; the tonal language rules; which real artefacts the series draws on.

**Does not decide:** episode content (`episodes.md`), look (`art-direction.md`), puzzle specifics (episode GDDs).

## Sofi

### Profile

About seven. First grade — she can just read, sounding out long words letter by letter. The game never states her age. Small, active, quick. Curious about everything and patient about nothing. Smart enough to start on any topic, too young to have been told what is "hard". Talks to Pluto constantly, mostly in questions.

Sofi is the lens (`vision.md`, decision 1). The world is bigger than her: doors are tall, switches are high, machines are enormous. This is a feature. It makes the abandoned places feel vast and gives Pluto a reason to help her reach, climb, and see.

### Behaviour rules

- Sofi moves first and thinks second. Her animation and lines should show impatience: bouncing, running ahead, touching things.
- Sofi asks. She does not explain. Explanation is Pluto's job or the environment's.
- Sofi is never scared for long. The places are lonely, not frightening. Her curiosity beats the dark within seconds.
- Sofi is never wrong in a way that punishes the player. When she rushes past a clue, the clue is still there.
- Sofi celebrates. Every restored system gets a reaction from her. Progress is felt through her.

### Voice

Short sentences. Present tense. Questions, exclamations, and naming things. No irony. Vocabulary a six-year-old owns, plus the new words she learns in the episode, which she then uses proudly and slightly wrong.

Proposed sample lines (to be accepted or rewritten by the creator):

```text
"Pluto! It's so dark. Why is it dark?"
"Is that a door? That's a BIG door."
"I hear it. Do you hear it? It's going... beep, beep, beep. Three!"
"Can you be a ladder? Be a ladder!"
"It's alive! Pluto, it's alive! I did that!"
"Where does it go? Can we go there? Let's go there."
```

## Pluto

### Nature

Made of living liquid metal. Can take any form: flow through a pipe, walk beside Sofi, fly, become a lens, a bridge, a key, a ladder, an antenna, a tuning fork. Reflective, calm, and quiet. Pluto is the patient one.

**Material rules (director, 2026-09-21).** Pluto conducts electricity. Pluto can pour off what a repair needs and grows back to full size in a few seconds; the poured piece stays as a **silver seam** in the repaired thing. Pluto is indestructible and self-repairing: forms change, nothing breaks. In-game the material is called "liquid metal" or simply Pluto; the word "quicksilver" is design shorthand only. The game never names mercury and never touches toxicity.

Pluto's origin is a held mystery (`vision.md`, open questions). Nothing in early episodes states where Pluto came from. Two readings stay possible: Pluto is simply Sofi's friend, or Pluto has something to do with the signal. Lines and events must not close either reading.

### The meeting

Sofi and Pluto do not start together. They meet in the opening of Episode 1 (`episode-01/scenes/opening.md`): a silver puddle inside a dark doorway that copies her taps — once, twice, three times — then rises. No shared language; repetition is the whole conversation. Sofi names it on the spot. Pluto does not speak in that scene. This is how the series' grammar (repetition, counting, answer) is taught before anyone knows it is a grammar.

### Roles

Pluto is the interface (`vision.md`, decision 7):

- **Companion.** Always near Sofi. Reacts, listens, waits.
- **Tool.** Puzzles are "what does Pluto need to become here?"
- **Translator.** Pluto hums tones, reads the observer's notes aloud, turns a diagram into a shape Sofi can walk around.
- **Hint system.** When the player stalls, Pluto shows a little more. Never a text box; a form, a gesture, a tone.
- **Narrator.** The previous observer's story is told through Pluto reading what was left behind.

### Form catalogue

Forms are unlocked per episode. Limits make puzzles: Pluto cannot do everything at once, and some forms need something from the environment first (light to become a lens, a current to become a conductor, a sound to become a tuning fork).

| Family | Forms (initial) | First episode | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Locomotion | Puddle (flow through gaps), Walker, Flyer | 1 | Puddle and Walker in Episode 1; Flyer later |
| Reach | Ladder, Bridge, Hand (press what Sofi cannot reach) | 1 | Ladder and Hand in Episode 1 |
| Tool | Key (fits a lock it has seen), Wheel, Conductor (bridge a broken circuit) | 1 | Conductor is the power puzzle in Episode 1 |
| Sense | Lens (focus light), Mirror, Ear (make a faint sound loud) | 1 | Ear introduces the signal |
| Speak | Tuning fork (hum a tone), Drum (rhythm), Chord (later) | 1 | Tuning fork is the first transmission |
| Show | Diagram (become the shape in the notes), Map | 1 | How Pluto explains without text |

Rules for adding a form: it must solve a puzzle in the episode that introduces it, be readable in silhouette, and fit the quicksilver material (see `art-direction.md`).

### Voice

Few words. Calm. Often just a tone or a form change instead of a line. When Pluto speaks, it is to slow Sofi down or to name what she has found.

Proposed sample lines:

```text
"Wait."
"Listen."
"Three. Then it starts again."
"That is a pattern, Sofi."
"I can be that."
"Someone was here before us."
```

## The spider

A resident of the observatory, met in the generator room (`episode-01/scenes/01-power.md`). Sits in the web across the cut cable; Pluto moves it gently aside. Appears somewhere in every later Episode 1 scene, always findable, never with a mechanic. Sofi counts its legs. Name held; Sofi's placeholder is "Eight". Whether the spider ever *acts* is deferred (director: "we can decide later; initially not"). One companion with mechanics stays the rule (`vision.md`, decision 7).

## The previous observer

Someone worked here decades ago. They heard the signal, tried to answer, and stopped one step too early. Never met, never named in Episode 1 (name held for later episodes).

What they left, by type:

- **Notes.** Short, handwritten, one idea each. Pluto reads them.
- **Diagrams.** Wiring, optics, sequences. Pluto can become them.
- **Marks on the world.** A switch labelled by hand, a mirror left at an angle, a count scratched on a wall.
- **The unfinished thing.** Every place has one: a transmission not sent, a job not run, a mirror not aligned.

The observer's arc across the series: from "they gave up" to "they left a trail on purpose". Decide the turn in `episodes.md`, not here.

## The Voice

The source of the signal. Never shown. Never fully understood. Never gives a final answer (`vision.md`, decision 4).

What the Voice does each episode: gives one more piece of structure. A count, a pattern, a direction, a frequency, a place to look next. The pieces accumulate into a language the player slowly learns.

What the Voice is not: not a character from any book or film; not hostile; not a puppet-master. It is patient in the same way Pluto is. Whether that similarity means anything is a held question.

**Name:** to be chosen by the creator. Constraints: pronounceable by a six-year-old, not a name from existing fiction, works as a word Sofi can shout.

## The tonal language

Invented here, owned here. Synthesised at runtime, never shipped as recordings.

### Rules v0

```text
One tone                 = one thing / one count
Same tone repeated N     = the number N          (Episode 1: count them)
Rising pitch             = a question
Falling pitch            = a statement
Pause                    = end of a word
Long pause               = end of a message
Chord (2–3 tones)        = a concept             (introduced in a later episode)
Number patterns          = the handshake         (counting first; primes when Sofi is ready)
```

### Episode 1 subset

Counting only. The signal repeats a short count. Sofi counts it. Pluto becomes a tuning fork and hums the count back. Silence. One clear tone returns, on a pitch that points somewhere (see `episode-01/GDD.md`).

### Growth across episodes

- Counting → patterns that repeat → patterns that grow → primes
- Single tones → rising/falling → chords
- Hearing → repeating → answering → asking
- Whether the player ever *composes* freely (the "choose" path) is decided per episode in `episodes.md`

### Sound design constraints

- Tones are pure or near-pure (sine, triangle, soft square) with a small set of timbres reserved for the Voice.
- Pluto's tuning-fork voice and the Voice must be distinguishable at once.
- Pitch range comfortable on laptop speakers.
- All of this is generated, so it costs almost nothing in bytes.

## Real artefacts

Real places are the heart of every episode. See `places.md` for the full map and `corpus/` for the reference material per place.

Episode 1 is locked: **VIRAC** (Ventspils International Radio Astronomy Centre), Irbene, Latvia. A former Soviet military installation turned radio astronomy research centre, deep in forest. Two steerable parabolic antennas (RT-32 and RT-16), a satellite laser ranging station, and a real Cold War history. The game's observatory is VIRAC, named as VIRAC, with the real geography and real instruments underneath its fictional story.

Real science facts used as story fuel:

| Real thing | Fact used | How it feeds the game |
|:---|:---|:---|
| VIRAC RT-32 antenna | 32-metre fully steerable parabolic dish; Cassegrain focus; used for radio astronomy and satellite tracking | The observatory's main instrument; Sofi wakes it |
| The site's Cold War origin | Built by the Soviet navy as a secret communications intercept station; later converted to science | The "abandoned military site turned science" is the real history; the observer's notes can reference the transition |
| VIRAC's forest location | Remote, surrounded by forest, access road through trees | The approach is real; the isolation is real |
| Satellite laser ranging | VIRAC operates a laser ranging station tracking satellites and space debris | A potential optional depth mechanic or a later episode seed |
| A 72-second narrowband signal, 1977 | Recorded once, never repeated, never explained | "A signal that never got an answer." The observer's obsession |
| A message sent to the stars, 1974 | Humans did send a deliberate message, encoded in numbers and pictures | The observer's unfinished transmission |
| The great dish that sent it collapsed in 2020 | A real lost observatory | The emotional truth: great instruments can fall silent |
| Records and plaques on deep-space probes | Humanity's actual universal handshakes: numbers, hydrogen, diagrams | The idea that counting is where contact starts |
| Space telescopes seeing very old light | Light takes time; looking far is looking back | Episode 5's lesson |

**Fiction boundary:** the site, its name, its coordinates, its instruments, and its history are real. That it is abandoned in the game world, that a lone observer worked there decades ago, and that the signal was heard there — that is ours. Every episode follows this pattern. The corpus (`corpus/`) separates fact from fiction.

## Ownership checks

Before anything ships, each of these must be true:

- No character name, design, voice, or catchphrase from existing fiction.
- The Voice's name is original.
- The tonal language is documented here as our own system.
- No film score, sound effect, or recognisable musical motif.
- Every asset has a provenance record (`asset-pipeline.md`).
