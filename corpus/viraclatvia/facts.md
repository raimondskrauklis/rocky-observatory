# VIRAC — Facts

All statements below are sourced. Source keys in brackets refer to `gallery.md`. Nothing here is invented. For what the game changes, see `fiction-boundaries.md`.

## Identity

- Name: Ventspils International Radio Astronomy Centre (VIRAC). Latvian: Ventspils Starptautiskais Radioastronomijas Centrs. [V1][V2]
- Location: Irbene, Ance parish, Ventspils municipality, Latvia. In pine forest near the Baltic coast, about 24–30 km north of Ventspils, by the Irbe river. [V1][LP][CW]
- Coordinates: approx. 57.553 N, 21.855 E. Elevation about 20 m. [KM]
- Operator: Engineering Research Institute VIRAC of Ventspils University of Applied Sciences. [V2]
- Status today: **operational**. RT-32 is used for radio astronomy and European VLBI Network observations. [V2][EVN]
- Soviet code name: **Zvyozdochka** (Звёздочка, "Little Star"); the town was called "Star" (Звезда). The road through the town was "Naberezhnaya" (Embankment). [V1][CW]
- Local nicknames for RT-32: "Saturn", "the Big Dish", "THA 400". [V1]

## Instruments

- **RT-32**: fully steerable parabolic antenna, 32 m diameter, about 600 tonnes, on a roughly 25 m concrete tower. Largest radio telescope in Northern Europe; described as eighth largest in the world. [V1][LP]
- RT-32 receivers cover 327 MHz, 1.6 GHz, 5 GHz and 12 GHz bands (327 MHz – 12 GHz). Secondary-focus vertex cabin refurbished; two VLBI data acquisition systems. First successful EVN fringe test 12 April 2012. [EVN]
- **RT-16**: fully steerable parabolic antenna, 16 m diameter. Reconstruction planned under the IKSA project (2011 ERDF grant). [V1][V2]
- An 8 m dish was also part of the original military complex. [CW]
- **LOFAR-LATVIA** station is part of VIRAC's instrument base. [V2]
- RT-32 mirror reconstruction completed June 2015; the parabola was lifted back onto its support tower. [V1]
- A 700 m underground tunnel exists on site; guided tours enter RT-32 and the tunnel. [CW]

## History

- Complex founded 10 June 1967 as a secret Soviet communications-intelligence station to intercept Western (satellite) communications. Antennas built 1967/1969–1973 by the Soviet military (navy). [V2][R1][CW]
- Predecessor units: Special Computing Centre No. 4 (1962); 649th Radio Intelligence Station / military unit 51429 (summer 1967). [V1]
- The town housed over 2,000 soldiers, scientists, and families: four apartment blocks with 260 flats, kindergarten, school, shops, sports centre, post office, on about 400 ha. [CW][KB]
- Summer 1993: Latvian scientists learned of the high-precision 32 m antenna before the Russian withdrawal and campaigned for its scientific use, with support from the Latvian and Russian academies, URSI, JIVE, Onsala, Max Planck Institute for Radio Astronomy, and others. [BA]
- On withdrawal, troops poured acid into the telescope motors, cut all cables, took the documentation, and destroyed much of the infrastructure. Both antennas were completely inoperable. [CW][EVN]
- 22 July 1994: the site was handed over to Latvia; VIRAC founded by the Latvian Academy of Sciences. The RT-32 parabola was the only large dish the previous owners left behind. [V1][V2][BA]
- 1994 onward: a group of enthusiasts from the Latvian Academy of Sciences restored RT-32 without documentation. Major reconstruction 2014–2019. [V1][V2][EVN]
- The military town was abandoned soon after 1993 and is now derelict; forest is reclaiming it. Free to walk around outside; buildings unsafe to enter. [KM][KB][LP]

## Science

- RT-32 does centimetre-wave radio astronomy and VLBI: it records faint radio signals from the sky with very precise timing so they can be combined with other telescopes across Europe. [EVN][V2]
- Applied work includes space debris radar and ionosphere sounding. [EVN]
- A radio telescope does not "look" with light. It collects radio waves in the dish, focuses them to a receiver at the focus, and records them as data. What it "hears" is invisible to the eye. [EVN]

## Geography

- Flat coastal pine forest; the dish rises above the treeline and is visible from a distance. [V1][LP]
- Access: off road P124 (Ventspils–Kolka). The approach passes the abandoned apartment blocks before reaching the telescope. [LP][KB]
- Weather (spring/autumn reference): daytime around 8–14 °C, wind up to ~18 km/h. Baltic coastal climate: overcast often, mist common, long dusks in summer, early dark in autumn/winter. [KM]
- Tours run until "it gets dark outside"; winter tours are special-arranged below 0 °C. [CW]
