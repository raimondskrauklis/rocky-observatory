# VIRAC — Fiction Boundaries

What the game keeps real, what it changes, what it invents. Any agent writing a scene reads this before writing.

**The most important boundary:** VIRAC is a living, working scientific institution with real people who restored it against the odds. The game portrays the place as it was in its broken years after 1994 — motors dead, cables cut, everything waiting — and it does so inside a child's dream. This is fiction with respect: the real story of its restoration is the spirit of the game, not something the game erases. The game never claims the real VIRAC is abandoned today.

**Tone rules (from the creative director):**

- No Cyrillic on screen. "Little Star" appears in the game's language (Latvian build: "Zvaigznīte"), never as Звёздочка.
- No Soviet nostalgia. The military past is a fact in the notes, not an aesthetic.
- No Chernobyl comparisons, no disaster imagery. Spooky is allowed and positive: cobwebs, spiders, moths at a lamp, an owl, wind in a broken window. Never dread.
- The place is broken, not ruined. Everything can be fixed. That is the point.

## Open boundary: dome and optical telescope

The base GDD (written before VIRAC was locked) has a dome, a shutter, and an optical telescope with an eyepiece. VIRAC has none of those; it is a radio dish. Options for the human:

1. Replace the dome/telescope chain with the dish: power → dish drive (the dish turns) → receiver → antenna. Sofi "looks" by listening. Honest to the place; teaches that telescopes are not only for eyes.
2. Keep a small optical telescope as a fictional addition (the observer's own instrument on the roof) so Sofi gets a first "look at the sky" moment before the dish teaches her about radio.
3. Both: the small optical telescope is the cartoon-telescope Sofi expected; the giant dish is the surprise.

Decide before the restoration chain is rewritten in the GDD.
